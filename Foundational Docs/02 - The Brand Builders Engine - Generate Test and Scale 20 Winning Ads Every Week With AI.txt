SECTION 6: THE DIRECT / INDIRECT SPECTRUM
Most people running AI image ads don't even know this map exists. Which is why they only run one type of ad and then complain they can't scale.
Here's the map.
Every image ad you can possibly run sits somewhere on this spectrum. On the right end, you have direct ads. 

On the left end, you have indirect ads. Most ads land somewhere in between, leaning one direction or the other.

Both types work. They just do different jobs and they target different audiences.

Direct Ads

Direct ads are exactly what they sound like. The person looking at the ad knows they're being sold to. There's no pretending.
The product is in the image. There's a benefit statement. Often a price. A clear call to action. Maybe some social proof.
Think of the most basic ad you can imagine: product on a clean background, "30% off this week," 5-star reviews, "shop now" button. That's a direct ad.
Direct ads work great in two specific contexts:
Retargeting. People who already know your brand. Already visited your site. Already added to cart. They're warm. They don't need to be pre-sold. They just need a reason to come back and buy.
Product-aware and most-aware audiences. People who already know your product exists or already know they need a solution like yours. The selling work is mostly done. Direct ads close.
The downside: direct ads usually don't scale to massive numbers on cold traffic. Cold audiences don't know your brand, don't know your product, and don't really care yet. Hitting them with "BUY MY THING" rarely works at scale because they haven't been warmed up.
You can absolutely run profitable direct ads. They just usually live in the lower-spend, higher-margin parts of your account.-857245905502057400623888

SECTION 7: SWIPE VS. ORIGINAL (THE 80/20 rule)
There are two ways to come up with image ads. Both work. Both have their place. Most people only do one.
Swipes

A "swipe" is when you find an ad that's already working. Usually, a competitor's, sometimes from a completely different niche, and you remake it for your brand.

This isn't copying. You're not stealing. (well some of you mfers are) You're taking a proven concept and adapting it. 

The structure works. The format works. The psychological angle works. You just fit it to your product, your customer, and your brand.

Swipes are powerful for one reason: you're reducing variables.

When you make an ad from scratch, you're guessing on every single dimension at once. The angle. The format. The headline. The visual style. The hook. Any of those guesses can be wrong, and most of them probably are.

When you swipe, the competitor already proved that combination of variables works. They spent the money to validate it. You're inheriting their data.
That's why swipes have a higher win rate. Not because they're better ads necessarily. Because you've removed half the guesswork before you even start.
Originals

Originals are concepts you create from scratch. No swipe. No template. Just you, your foundational docs, and an LLM helping you ideate.

Originals have a slightly lower win rate than swipes. You're back to guessing on more variables.

But here's the thing: when an original works, it really works. Because nobody else has it.

Your top-scaling ads, the ones doing $50K, $100K, $200K in spend per month, those are almost always originals. Swipes get you started. Originals are where the moat is.

Why? Because nobody can swipe what you haven't published yet. The moment your original starts working at scale, every competitor with an ad spy tool is going to copy it. But you got there first. You collected the data. You scaled it. By the time they catch up, you've already moved to the next angle.
This is the actual game. Without originals you’ll always be (AT BEST) second.

The 80/20

Here's the rule I follow, especially in the early stages:
80% swipes. 20% originals.
When you're hunting for your winning sales message, swipes get you there faster. Lower variance. Higher win rate. More signal per dollar spent.
The 20% originals are your moonshots. Most of them won't work. But the ones that do are the ones nobody can copy yet. That's where your scaling ceiling lives.
Once you find your winning sales message, once a few ads are consistently profitable and you know what's resonating, the ratio flips.
Then it becomes 80% originals, 20% swipes. Because at that point you understand the message at a deep level. You can crank out 10 variations of an original concept all built around your winning message, and most of them will work.
But you have to earn that. You don't start there.

A Quick Note on Originality
Most people in the e-com space have about four brain cells combined. Almost everyone is just copying what their competitors are doing. Same products. Same angles. Same ads.

This is good news for you.

If you can use AI as a second brain to generate genuinely original concepts. Ideas that aren't already running in your niche. You have a moat that almost nobody else has bothered to build.
Originality used to require talent. Now it requires a Claude subscription and a willingness to actually think for 20 minutes.
Most people won't do it. Be the one who does.

A Note on Prompting
If your text comes out misspelled, your layout looks weird, or the brand details are off, that's not Nano Banana Pro's fault. That's your prompting.
These models can generate near-flawless images and text if the prompt is detailed enough. They can't extract everything from a reference image. They can get close, but if you want pixel-level control, you have to specify it in the prompt.
When in doubt, give Claude more context. Brand colors. Font style. Copy hierarchy. Background details. Lighting. Mood. The more specific you are, the better the output.

The Image Ad Hook Breakdown
For image ads specifically, three things make a hook work:
Visual Contrast (the scroll-stopper layer)
The image has to look different enough from the rest of the feed that someone's thumb pauses for half a second. Not "different and beautiful" necessarily. Sometimes it's "different and ugly." Sometimes "different and confusing." Whatever breaks the pattern.
This is why some of the best indirect ads are intentionally a little weird-looking. They don't blend in.

Headline Copy on the Image (70-80% of the work)
The single most important element of any image ad. The headline either makes them want to read more or it doesn't. Copy carries the load.
If your image is gorgeous but your headline is "save 20% today," your ad is dead. If your image is mid but your headline is "I tried this for 7 days and my doctor was speechless," people stop and read.

Implied Curiosity (the click trigger)
The best image ads make the viewer ask a question they need the next click to answer. They don't tell the whole story. They tell just enough that you have to keep going.
"She's 57 from Norway. Here's why she has zero belly fat." → You want to know why.
"Please stop buying oral B12 for neuropathy." → You want to know what to buy instead.
"My dog stopped slipping on hardwood after I did this." → You want to know what.
Curiosity is the click. Bake it in.

SECTION 9: GRADUATING TO VIDEO ADS
Let me be very clear about something before we get into video.
If you're just starting with AI ads, do not start with video. Start with images.
I know video ads look cooler. I know your favorite gurus on Twitter are showing off their hyperrealistic AI UGC and animated VSLs and making it look like the only way to win in 2026 is to be making cinematic 30-second video ads.
Don't fall for it.
Image ads are how you find your winning sales message. They're cheap. They're fast. You can test 20 angles in a week for less than $500. Each image is a hypothesis: "does this message resonate with this market?"
Once you've found the message… that's when you go invest the time and money to scale into video.
Trying to find your winning message via video first is just expensive guessing. You'll burn 5-10x more money than you needed to.
Image first. Find the message. Then video.

Why Video Once You Have the Message
A few reasons video matters once you've cracked the message:
It scales further on cold traffic without saturating as fast. Image ads can saturate quickly on Meta. I don't know exactly why, but it's true. Video ads can run profitably on cold traffic for much longer at much higher daily budgets.
Higher conversion rates on average. Not always, but typically. Because video gets more of the viewer's attention. Their eyes aren't bouncing around the way they do with an image. You have them in isolation for 15, 30, 60 seconds. The longer someone interacts with your brand, the higher the conversion rate. This is just how it works.
Audio adds a dimension. Voiceover. Character voice. Background music. None of that exists in an image. Audio amplifies the message and makes it stick.
So once you have the message, video lets you scale it.

You're not stealing their script. You're using their structure. Their pacing. Their format. The thing they proved already works.

Claude rewrites the script in your brand's voice with your psychological angles. Read through it. Adjust anything that sounds off. Approve the final version.

4a) Generate the starting frame as an image.
Use Claude (image project from earlier) to write a text-to-image prompt for the first frame of the clip. Paste into higgsfield with Nano Banana Pro. Generate 4 variations. Pick the strongest one.
4b) Image-to-video.
Take that starting frame and run it through Kling 3 or Veo 3.1 (image-to-video mode) with a separate video prompt that describes what should happen during the clip, the motion, the camera movement, the action, the duration.
This second prompt matters. A lot of people generate a great starting frame and then write a vague image-to-video prompt and end up with weird, off-brand motion. Be specific. What's moving? In what direction? At what speed? Where does the clip end?
Repeat for every clip in your storyboard.I will say this about the prompt… 
simplicity scales.
There is such a thing as too much when it comes to prompting for videos.
Step 5: Edit
Open Premiere, CapCut, or whatever you use. Drop in your clips in order. Add captions. Trim where needed. Add a few cuts to keep the pacing tight. Layer in voiceover or character audio.
Ship it.
The whole process, start to finish, takes me about 30-45 minutes per video ad once you've done it a few times. Compare that to the 3-7 days it used to take with a production team.

The Video Hook Breakdown
FACT: 90% of your video ad's success comes down to the first 3-5 seconds. The hook.
Three components, ranked by importance:
Copy / Headline (70-80% of the hook)
The text overlay or spoken hook in the first 3 seconds. This is the same as image ads, copy is doing most of the work. If your hook copy is weak, the prettiest scroll-stopper in the world won't save you.
Examples that work:
"Here's what doctors never tell you about why you're tired."
"I woke up one morning and everything looked like there were two of them."
"Please stop buying [common product] for [problem]."
Each one creates curiosity, calls out a specific person, or discredits the obvious solution.

Scroll-Stopper Clip (the visual)
The first frame people see. It needs to be weird enough or compelling enough that someone's thumb pauses.
Some of my best scroll-stoppers are intentionally bizarre. A character whose legs are melting because she's so tired. A confused-looking dog. A weird animation. 
The viewer should look at the first frame and go "what the fuck am I looking at?" and that confusion buys you the next 2 seconds to deliver the hook.

Audio (least important of the three)
Voiceover, character voice, background music. Matters, but not as much as the other two. If your copy and scroll-stopper hit, decent audio is enough.
Once the hook works:
Body sells the mechanism - why your product is different, what's actually happening, why they should believe you
CTA closes - clear next step, link in bio, tap below, whatever
That's the whole structure. Hook, body, CTA.

The Video Tool Stack (with Specific Use Cases)
Each tool has a job. Pick the right one for the moment.
Nano Banana Pro - every starting frame. Don't use anything else for this. Best in class.
Kling 3.0 - best balance of quality and cost for image-to-video. Default for testing. This is the workhorse of my video creation.
Veo 3.1 - cheaper and faster than Kling, slightly worse prompt adherence. Solid for high-volume testing when you're trying to crank out 20 video concepts at once. Strong specifically for image-to-video mode.
Seedance 2.0 - most realistic, most expensive video model available. Do not use this for testing. The credits will eat you alive.
The only use case for Seedance: take your top 1-2 winning videos that have already proven themselves at scale, and remake them with Seedance for an even higher-fidelity version that pushes top-of-funnel reach further.
In other words: test with Kling/Veo, scale with Seedance.
Higgsfield - the aggregator that lets you run all of these in one place. I'm not affiliated with them. I just use them constantly. It's nice having every model in one tab.
Realism Is Not the Goal
This is where most people running AI video ads are getting it wrong.
The whole "AI is so realistic now!!!" conversation is over. We crossed the realism threshold a year ago. Sora 2, Veo, Kling, they all hit a level of realism that makes "is this AI?" basically irrelevant.
So if everyone has access to realistic AI video, realism is not your moat anymore.
What's actually winning right now? Creative format diversity.
Some of the top-performing ads on my account in the last 30 days have been:
Animation-style ads with cartoon characters
Weird scroll-stoppers that look intentionally fake (legs melting, dogs floating, etc.)
AI versions of formats that look like they came out of 1995 infomercials
Here's the move that almost nobody is making: stop looking forward, look backward.
Don't go on Twitter or Instagram looking for "the latest AI ad formats." Those people are 6 months to multiple years behind. They're showing you formats that are already saturated.
Go backwards. Watch TV commercials from the 80s, 90s, 2000s. Watch infomercials. Watch QVC. 
The formats that ran on television for decades, those are the ones nobody is testing on Meta right now. They worked for 30 years on TV. They'll work on Meta too. And nobody else in your niche is doing them because they're all chasing the latest shiny AI trend.
Stop trying to make the most realistic AI video on Earth. Make the most attention-grabbing one. Those are different things.

SECTION 10: THE COPY FRAMEWORKS THAT WIN

"I Was Just Like You, But Worse" (link ad)
The most powerful first-person testimonial structure I've seen.
The character in the ad has the same problem as the customer… but worse. Way worse. They went through a more extreme version of the journey. Tried more things. Failed harder. Got more lost.
Then they found the solution. And it worked.
The customer watching thinks: "If she fixed it, and she had it way worse than me, then this will definitely work for me."
The thyroid eye disease ad is a perfect example. The character couldn't drive. Couldn't read. Couldn't work. Her life was falling apart. She tried multiple doctors. Multiple specialists. All told her nothing was wrong.
The viewer watching has maybe mild fatigue. Maybe some brain fog. Maybe nothing as extreme as "everything looks like there are two of it."
But the implied math is: "If this woman with severe symptoms found relief, my mild symptoms will definitely respond to this."
This framework is gold for any health, beauty, or wellness product. Find the most extreme version of your customer's pain. Tell that story.
*Note: This type of ad must be based on a real testimonial or customer experience.

The "Without" Word
Top 5 most powerful words in direct response copywriting.
The structure: "How to [desired outcome] WITHOUT [the obvious solution everyone hates]."
"How to lose weight without giving up carbs."
"How to clear your skin without prescription medication."
"How to grow your hair back without minoxidil side effects."
The word "without" does two jobs at once:
Promises the outcome the customer wants
Discredits the alternative they've already tried (or are scared to try)
Even when you don't literally use the word "without," the same logic applies. Your job in any ad is to position your mechanism by killing the alternatives. Make the customer believe nothing else works and then present your solution which leads me too…

Discrediting Common Solutions
Related but distinct from "without."
Most customers in your market have tried something already. They tried supplements. They tried the diet. They tried the device. They tried the prescription. None of it worked. Or worse…it worked for a while, then stopped.
Smart copy acknowledges this. "You've tried [common solution]. Here's why it didn't work."
Then you reveal your unique mechanism. The thing your competitors don't have. The reason your product solves the problem when nothing else did.
The thyroid ad does this perfectly. "Every doctor told me it had nothing to do with my thyroid."
Implied: the obvious experts in this space were wrong. Here's what they missed.
This positions your product not just as another option… it positions it as the option, because everything else has failed.

Specificity Over Generality
This is the difference between mediocre copy and copy that converts.
VAGUE
"My labs were normal."
"It happened really fast."
"Most people don't realize this."
SPECIFIC
"My TSH was 1.8."
"Within 9 days, I noticed a difference."
"83% of women over 40 have this and don't know it."

Specifics create believability. Numbers, dates, percentages, exact phrases your customer would recognize. These make the story feel real. 
Generalities feel like marketing. Specifics feel like truth.
When you're writing or reviewing AI-generated copy, hunt for vague language and replace it with specifics from your foundational docs.

Why This Matters for AI Ads Specifically
Here's the thing about AI-generated copy: by default, it's average. Models are trained on the entire internet, which means they default to the most common, most generic version of every sentence.
Your job is to push past the average.
When you use Claude to generate ad copy, your default output will sound like every other AI-generated ad in your niche. Same structures. Same phrasing. Same weak hooks.
The way you push past it is by:
Feeding Claude rich foundational docs (so it has specific language to pull from)
Asking it to apply specific frameworks (like the four above)
Iterating, never accept the first output. Push back. "Make this more specific. Discredit this alternative. Use the 'without' structure here."

The frameworks above are the patterns to push toward. The more your ad copy sounds like the generic AI default, the worse it'll perform. The more it sounds like a real human with real pain telling a real story, the more it scales.

Paste into Higsfield with the same settings
Generate

Final 30 minutes.
Go through everything you generated. For each concept, pick the strongest 1-2 variations. Kill the rest.

Visual contrast: does this break the scroll?
Headline: is the copy doing the work? Or is it generic?
